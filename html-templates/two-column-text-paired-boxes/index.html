Engagement · Phase 1 baseline

The review isolated five drivers; resolving the first two releases most of the deferred benefit.

Background & mandate

The Town has been negotiating with the County for shared services since 2019 without a documented service-level basis.

Deloitte was engaged to assess the Town’s technology controls, processes, policies and underlying infrastructure against comparable municipalities, and to state whether the current IT operating model meets business needs. At present 71% of the Town’s IT operations are outsourced to the County of Oxford, and management is seeking direction on a future model.1

The assessment covered five areas: applications and infrastructure, governance and strategy, information security, and disaster recovery. Fieldwork ran 14 working days across two sites and included 21 interviews, network testing with QualysGuard, and licence reconciliation against the 2014 audit.2

What this phase had to answer
  1. Future-state IT operating structure and governance model — who owns service, licence and change decisions, and against which SLA.
  2. Directional hardware and software requirements — the refresh envelope, sized to a 3-year cycle rather than last year’s budget.
  3. Sustainable IT funding model — capital versus operating split, with the Town’s reserve position stated as a range.
  4. Disaster recovery and business-continuity plan — including the redundancy gap left open by the 2014 audit.
Source: 1 Town of Tillsonburg, IT Strategy Review — Council Update, Deloitte LLP (Jan 2014), pp. 9–11. 2 Fieldwork log 06/12–06/27/2014; interview index Appendix C; licence counts per Microsoft audit letter of 2014-06-20.
