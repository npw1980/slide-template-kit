07 | Options appraisal

Fully in-house IT is the recommended operating model

Four options scored against risk, service impact and five-year cost1 — none is free, one is defensible.

OptionRiskBenefitsCostDecision

1Status quo

Keep the shared-service relationship with the county; no formal IT function.

 High No disruption; current staffing intact. Retains informal escalation paths that already miss service targets. None on paper — absorbed as staff overtime and deferred refresh cycles.

Not viable

Fails 3 of 5 council objectives

2Fully in-house IT

Develop a 4-person municipal IT department with direct council reporting.

 Moderate Full control of devices, infrastructure and roadmap; clear accountability to the council and residents. Hiring and training premium; $1.4M/yr fully loaded for a 4-person team.

Recommended

Phase in FY27, first 18 months

3County contract + governance

Maintain the county relationship with a dedicated IT steering board and SLA.

 Low Leverages county scale and negotiated licences; predictable run-rate; risk shared with the county. Low incremental cost; 3-yr commitment caps local change queue and roadmap control.

Fallback

Revisit at FY30 county contract renewal

4Outsource to private provider

Transfer all IT to a managed provider under a fixed-fee contract.

 High Rapid adoption of leading practices; provider owns lifecycle and on-call burden. Few credible vendors within region — single-source exposure; exit costs unclear.

Not short-term

No qualified vendor met RFP bar

Source: capability assessment workshops, May 2026; five-year TCO model v4 (present-value basis, 3.2% discount); IT council minutes Q1–Q3 2026.1
