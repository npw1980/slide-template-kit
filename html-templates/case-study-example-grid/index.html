Case studies — who is already moving

Companies that embed decarbonisation in core decisions, not just targets, cut emissions fastest

Across the 109 leading-company sample, the firms outperforming the median had one trait in common: carbon choices sat inside capital, procurement and product decisions—a not in a standalone sustainability plan.

Natura

SBTi-validated goal of 90% absolute reduction across its supply chain (2019–2026), enforced through supplier scorecards and science-based procurement standards. Grid emissions intensity fell 19% vs 2020, with 15% of scope 1–2 already abated via internal carbon pricing.

Siemens

Targets 50% absolute cut by 2030, validated by SBTi and aligned to TCFD. 76% of scope 1–2 energy now renewable; the fleet electrification programme and an internal CO2 price anchor the remaining gap to 2030.

CVS Health

Reweighted capital towards respiratory and cardiovascular care, cutting environmental impact 50% vs 2019 after closing coal-fired plants and shifting 78% of energy spend to renewables plus on-site generation.

HP

SBTi 1.5°C goal: 89% absolute reduction in scope 1–3 vs 2019, achieved largely through supplier engagement—87,000 tCO2 avoided in the product supply chain, with a 1.5°-aligned product take-back programme.

Suntory

Energy-saving measures cut scope 1–2 emissions 28% vs 2018 while production grew; 78% renewable electricity across plants, and heat-recovery retrofits hold unit emissions flat as volumes rise.

Mahindra & Mahindra

A carbon-neutral goal by 2040, validated as near-term SBTi: 55% renewable energy by 2025, 19% efficiency gain per unit output, and supplier decarbonisation embedded in the new-vehicle development gate.

Source: Accenture analysis; SBTi registry and company sustainability disclosures, 2023.a Leading-company sample, n=109; internal carbon pricing counted where disclosed. 36 | CASE STUDIES
