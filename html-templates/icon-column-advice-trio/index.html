Maturity spectrum — where you are

The next move differs by how far each company has already come on the journey

Companies just getting started:

Set targets, create milestones and start measuring progress

The first step is a defensible baseline, not a public statement. Companies that map scope 1 and 2 emissions across owned operations before announcing, and that validate the target against an external standard, report faster progress than peers who announce before they measure.a Governance follows measurement: one named executive owner, a monthly reporting cadence, and a standing item on the board agenda keep the target inside the business rather than inside the communications plan. The second year is the hard one: once the low-cost options are exhausted, progress depends on whether the target survives a budget cycle, and companies that report monthly are far better placed to defend it than those reporting annually into a dashboard nobody reads.

Companies already on the journey:

Prioritize investments and harness efficiencies across the enterprise

Once a target exists, the work moves to costed initiatives with owners and dates. The highest-return programmes pair an internal price signal with a capital allocation rule, so low-cost abatement is executed before expensive options.b Firms in this group typically run on three numbers: marginal abatement cost, spend against plan, and the remaining gap to target. Everything else is commentary. What stops firms here is not analysis; it is that the capital committee keeps funding projects without an owner, and the abatement that is cheapest on paper is the slowest to arrive.

Companies that are advanced:

Place holder bets, innovate, invest and collaborate across sectors and value chains

Advanced companies treat what they do not control as part of the plan. They work suppliers up to disclosed data, place small bets on technologies that are not yet economic, and collaborate where one firm cannot move the curve alone.c The trade-off is real: this work accepts higher execution risk and longer payback than the low-cost measures already booked. Disclosure follows, not leads — advanced firms publish against a framework their peers recognize rather than inventing one, because the credibility of the claim is what buys access to the ones who have to sign up to it.

Source: Firm research team survey of 420 listed companies, emissions self-reported by management and validated where an external assurance statement exists; segment assignment on disclosure, target validation and reporting cadence, unweighteda,b,c. Percentages do not sum to 100 because companies moving between segments are counted at the survey date.
