Findings 02 · Target-setting

Companies holding two targets cut emissions three times faster than single-target peers.

More net zero targets are proliferating. A third of the world’s largest companies listed in the 2022 analysis now have a net zero target in place, up from 17% a year earlier, and 62% have set an interim 2030 milestone alongside it.1 The pace is not uniform: firms in Europe set targets 1.4× more often than firms in the same revenue band elsewhere, and disclosed a median of two decarbonisation levers per company.

Targets with a plan beat targets without one. Companies that published a transition plan alongside the target reduced emissions 9.8% per year against 3.1% for target-only peers over the same period.2 The gap is wider in heavy industry, where a plan is the difference between a capex commitment and a statement of intent.

The cost of delay is now priced. Firms that deferred capital decisions past 2023 carry a median 11% higher abatement cost per tonne than peers of the same size that committed early, and 29% of that premium is locked into supply contracts that run past the interim milestone.2

But on current trends, only 8% of companies with a net zero target reach it by 2050, and the shortfall is concentrated in exactly the sectors that matter most.

Where the gap sits. Electricity, transport and heavy industry account for 71% of covered emissions yet hold only 38% of the transition plans. Renewables and efficiency carry 84% of the committed investment, and almost none of the plan capital is directed at process change.3

What separates the fast movers: a named owner per lever, an internal carbon price above USD 50/tCO2e, and compensation linked to the interim milestone rather than to the 2050 date.

Late movers buy optionality, not progress. Where capital is still uncommitted, 63% of the gap is closed with purchased offsets and renewable certificates rather than with process change — accounting treatments that move the target date by less than four months on a science-based basis.3

Method
Sample: 2,100 companies ≥ USD 1bn revenue across 24 markets and 11 sectors, screened for a public net zero or carbon-neutral commitment. Targets and plans captured from corporate disclosures and CDP responses between 2021-04-01 and 2022-12-31; emissions on a market-based scope 1–2 basis, restated for divestments.1 Sector mapping is per GHG Protocol, not by SIC code, so 4% of the population differs from the 2021 edition.2
