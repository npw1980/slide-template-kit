02 | Development: stakeholder views

Four stakeholder groups each blame someone else for the same slow access to trials

Interview-based, 62 stakeholders across 4 groups, fieldwork Q3 2015
1 Industry views n = 18
  • Consensus that the UK has a strong capability to conduct medical research and development
  • There is a lack of incentives and recognition for clinicians and nurses to work on clinical trials
  • Interviewers mentioned increasing cost and effort to set up trials with duplicated contract negotiation
  • Misalignment of interests between stakeholders — value lost to duplicated development efforts
2 HMG / ALB views n = 14
  • Industry is reluctant to engage NICE and others on product development and evidence collection early
  • Fees are a potential barrier to early-stage companies seeking advice, particularly for SMEs
  • Knowledge and technologies are not shared between industry and other organisations pre-competitively
  • Risk of early-stage development is better shared than it currently is
3 NHS views n = 19
  • Respondents cite a lack of specialised R&D nurses, clinician capacity and time for conducting trials
  • Revenue generated by trial participation does not reflect the workload required
  • Payment based on recruitment number, not the complexity of the study
  • Misalignment between local and global study-sponsor offices — multiple negotiation processes
4 Charity views n = 11
  • Patient groups feel the development process is too long and the paradigm does not fit rare, highly stratified therapies
  • Industry cannot invest without reasonable returns, especially for small patient populations
  • Late-stage access to trials is the binding constraint, not recruitment capacity
So whatThe disagreement is not about the science — it is about who pays for the trial setup and who owns the duplicated effort. That is where the intervention belongs.2
