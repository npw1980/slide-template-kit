Executive summary

Firms that pair a strong risk core with the ability to move faster than their portfolio changes are outperforming peers on both volatility and growth — but only where the CRO sits inside transformation decisions, not downstream of them.

Risk is no longer a single portfolio problem. CROs describe their mandate as covering cyber, climate, and technology risk alongside the traditional financial book, and 5 of 10 of the largest emerging risks now sit outside the actuarial function entirely.1

Capability gaps concentrate mid-stack. Data quality, tooling, and specialist talent are the constraints named consistently across firms, while governance — the cheapest layer to upgrade — is nearly universally claimed and rarely demonstrated under stress.

Transformation is the CRO’s agenda. Firms that involve risk leadership in transformation design report faster capital release and fewer product-level surprises; where the risk function arrives late, first-year remediation costs dominate everything the initiative saves.1
