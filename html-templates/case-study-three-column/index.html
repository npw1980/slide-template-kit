Case study: Pharma

Adoption of new stroke-prevention drugs stays limited despite clear clinical and cost benefits

Background

Non-Vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulants (NOACs) were approved by NICE in 2011, with a 2016 update adding the first stroke-prevention option beyond warfarin.

Guidance sits with NICE, but uptake is decided by local formularies — 21 systems reviewed, no single gate.

European Society of Cardiology guidance already recommends them; the gap between recommendation and practice is the story.

Issues

Clinicians remain wary of the 2016 safety signal and the absence of long-term stroke data beyond 36 months.

The 90-day formulary rule delays adoption; prescribers wait for a second opinion before the first is booked.

NICE concluded cost-effectiveness, but primary-care budget holders price the drug per patient, not per pathway.

Challenges

Budget silos between secondary and primary care mean the party that pays is not the party that benefits.

No accountability mechanism for outcomes — no registry tracks who prescribed what, and what happened after.

Transparency gap: NICE appraisals lag real-world evidence by 18–24 months, feeding the caution.

Implication

Reimbursement bodies should fund an outcomes registry and an early-access pathway — not another HTA review.
